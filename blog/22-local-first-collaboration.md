# Building a local-first collaborative tool

This is my own personal journey into a movement I think matters – local-first software and a tutorial for how to build a collaborative drawing application using YJs

I first heard of local-first software while at my 12 week batch at Recurse. 
I had this idea about building a digital audio time capsule where I would record a message for my future self, set a date in the future, then on that date I would receive the message.  It seemed simple to build, but as is often the case, it became more complex the deeper i dug into a solution.

Then by chance I saw a post by Brian Hicks who was writing the first drafts of a book on local-first. A morning’s google search later I found a 2019 article from [Ink & Switch - You own your own data, in spite of the cloud.](https://www.inkandswitch.com/local-first/)

I felt hopeful. I felt energized. I felt I needed to be part of this movement. I also felt very late to the party.

## Tenants of Local-first software
1. No spinners - meaning no waiting for requests.
2. Your work is not trapped on one device.
3. The network is optional.
4. Seamless collaboration with your colleagues.
5. The long now
6. Security and privacy by default
7. You retain ultimate ownership and control

#7 peaked my interest - yes! this should be a given. It forced me to reflect on all the companies that own my data today. Google (drive, docs, photos, gmail), Notion. Years ago I shed all of my social media accounts.
